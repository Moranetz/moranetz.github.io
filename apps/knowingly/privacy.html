Knowingly — Privacy Policy

Effective: 2026-05-15 · Developer: Marion Moranetz

Knowingly runs no backend. Nothing leaves your phone except the message bubbles you choose to send inside iMessage. There is no account, no email collected, no analytics SDK, no advertising SDK.

What is collected

  • Markets, bets, calibration scores: stored locally on your device using App Groups (group.com.moranetz.knowingly) so the main app and the iMessage extension can share state.
  • Friend names: typed by you when you set up your group. Stored locally.
  • Subject opt-in receipts and votes: stored locally per device.

None of this is transmitted to me, my servers, or any third party. There are no servers.

What is sent

When you tap "Send" inside the iMessage extension, an MSMessage carrying the market question, subject name, group name, and the snapshot probability at send time is inserted into the iMessage conversation. From that point Apple's Messages infrastructure handles delivery — Apple's privacy policy governs the transit.

What recipients see

If a recipient has Knowingly installed, tapping the bubble opens the extension and displays the market with their own device-local state. If they don't have it installed, they see the bubble image with caption only.

Third-party services

Knowingly does not use third-party analytics (Mixpanel, Amplitude, Segment), advertising SDKs (AdMob, Facebook Audience Network, Unity Ads), off-device crash reporters (Sentry, Crashlytics, Bugsnag), or tracking SDKs. If this ever changes, this policy and the app's release notes will say so.

Children's privacy (COPPA)

Knowingly is rated 12+ and does not knowingly collect personal information from children under 13. If you have a concern, contact me at the address below.

Your rights

  • Delete your data: delete the app; all locally stored data goes with it.
  • Stop tracking: there is nothing to stop — Knowingly does not track you.

For users under GDPR, CCPA, LGPD, or similar: because no personal data is collected on any server I control, there is no controller-side data to access, port, or erase. Local data is governed by Apple's iOS standards and your control of your device.

Changes

Updates get a new "Effective" date here and a note in the next App Store release notes.
