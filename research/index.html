The programme

Neurotech has hardware builders and it has theorists. Almost nobody turns a felt, subjective phenomenon into a number somebody else can check. Each paper here is an instrument pointed at something a field currently asserts without measuring: what a night mode actually does to you, what makes a screen feel good, what a brain-computer interface never measures about the person using it, and why any of these regularities exist to be exploited in the first place.

Standing rule

Every citation is graded, and the grade sits on the page beside the claim. VERIFIED means a primary source was opened and says what it is cited for. CONTESTED means the finding is real, disputed, and the dispute is named. Sources that could not be verified are printed as unverified rather than dropped, corrections to earlier drafts are marked in the text rather than swapped out, and the folklore each field repeats is quarantined by name.

Paper 01 · Light

Filters Don’t Work. Palettes Do.

Night mode is a marketing claim, not a measurement. A per-pixel melanopic-dose instrument, pointed at the interfaces people trust before bed.

Kindle’s sepia mode doses at 76% of white, because it is bright. Warmth is a decoy and luminance is what matters. A red filter strong enough to cut dose 21× collapses body text to APCA Lc 9.7, so there is nothing usable in between.

Paper 02 · Aesthetics

Measured Neuroaesthetics

“This interface feels premium” is treated as ineffable taste. It decomposes into eight mechanisms, each tied to a property you can already instrument, plus a short list of folklore to exclude.

The one mechanism that is not a static pixel property, the split between liking and wanting, is exactly what separates good design from a dark pattern. Aesthetic quality and manipulation are read on the same axis in opposite directions.

Paper 03 · Neural interfaces

The Unmeasured Half of the Neural Interface

Two decades of brain-computer interface work optimised the decoder, measured almost entirely by Information Transfer Rate.

ITR has no term for effort, fatigue, dropout, or trust. Meanwhile 15 to 30% of users cannot reliably control a given motor-imagery BCI regardless of decoder quality, and part of that is an interface artifact rather than a biological ceiling.

Paper 04 · Ultimate causes

The Evolved Substrate of Human Influenceability

The paper underneath the other three. Modern persuasion does not defeat reasoning, it goes around it, by showing evolved motivational systems stimuli those systems never met in the environment that shaped them.

The exploitable regularities are not bugs. They are adaptations running outside their design environment, which is why naming the system under a technique turns “this feels manipulative” into a question with an answer: which system is being triggered, and is the trigger honest?

In progress

The Closing Evidence Atlas

A pre-registered PRISMA 2020 systematic review and Bayesian meta-analysis of 39 named influence techniques. 11,785 records screened across seven public databases. 15 of the 39 techniques have zero peer-reviewed empirical studies behind them. Methods, data and screening decisions are public at github.com/Moranetz/closing-evidence-atlas. The production analysis run has not landed, so no headline effect size is claimed here yet.
