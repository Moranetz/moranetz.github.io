Standing rule

Every citation is graded, and the grade sits on the page beside the claim. VERIFIED means a primary source was opened and says what it is cited for. CONTESTED means the finding is real, disputed, and the dispute is named. Sources that could not be verified are printed as unverified rather than dropped, corrections to earlier drafts are marked in the text rather than swapped out, and the folklore each field repeats is quarantined by name.

Defense · Field Notes on the Operator Layer

The people who run the machines

Militaries can now buy drones, robots and targeting software faster than they can prepare the people who run them. These four open-source reviews follow that gap through Ukraine, the Iran war and the U.S. Army’s own statements. Every claim carries an evidence tier and a link, and every paper ends with its limits.

No. 1 · Operators

340,000 Drones, 3,000 Dedicated Pilots

The Army plans about 340,000 small drones over two years and projects 2,000 to 3,000 soldiers whose full-time job is flying drones.

RUSI found that 60 to 80 percent of Ukrainian FPV drones fail to reach their target and names operator skill as one of two causes. The paper closes with six measures any training program could report, and none currently does.

No. 2 · The kill chain

One Targeting Decision Every 3.6 Seconds

Software let a 20-person targeting cell match the output of a 2,000-person cell from the Iraq War, and the Army now wants a thousand decisions an hour.

Patriot’s 2003 fratricides and thirty years of human-factors research point the same way. Training is necessary, and automation bias survives it, so part of the fix has to live in the system.

Open-source review v1.0 · 12 references
No. 3 · Cost exchange

The $4 Million Answer to a $35,000 Drone

A Patriot interceptor costs about $4 million. A Shahed attack drone costs tens of thousands.

The Iran war used up half to two-thirds of some U.S. interceptor stocks. Ukraine’s $1,000 interceptor drones narrow the gap, and each still needs a person flying its final seconds.

No. 4 · Industry

Ukraine Can Build Three Times What It Can Afford

Ukraine’s spare defense production capacity is estimated at €30.5 billion, and procurement uses about 30 percent of it.

In every partnership now running, the partner brings the money or the market and Ukraine keeps the design. A drone supply chain outside China is the piece neither side has built.

Neuro · The measurement layer

Instruments for felt experience

The programme

Neurotech has hardware builders and it has theorists. Almost nobody turns a felt, subjective phenomenon into a number somebody else can check. Each paper here is an instrument pointed at something a field currently asserts without measuring: what a night mode actually does to you, what makes a screen feel good, what a brain-computer interface never measures about the person using it, and why any of these regularities exist to be exploited in the first place.

Paper 01 · Light

Filters Don’t Work. Palettes Do.

Night mode is a marketing claim, not a measurement. A per-pixel melanopic-dose instrument, pointed at the interfaces people trust before bed.

Kindle’s sepia mode doses at 76% of white, because it is bright. Warmth is a decoy and luminance is what matters. A red filter strong enough to cut dose 21× collapses body text to APCA Lc 9.7, so there is nothing usable in between.

Instrument · melanopic.py, screen_dose.py
Paper 02 · Aesthetics

Measured Neuroaesthetics

“This interface feels premium” is treated as ineffable taste. It decomposes into eight mechanisms, each tied to a property you can already instrument, plus a short list of folklore to exclude.

The one mechanism that is not a static pixel property, the split between liking and wanting, is exactly what separates good design from a dark pattern. Aesthetic quality and manipulation are read on the same axis in opposite directions.

Paper 03 · Neural interfaces

The Unmeasured Half of the Neural Interface

Two decades of brain-computer interface work optimised the decoder, measured almost entirely by Information Transfer Rate.

ITR has no term for effort, fatigue, dropout, or trust. Meanwhile 15 to 30% of users cannot reliably control a given motor-imagery BCI regardless of decoder quality, and part of that is an interface artifact rather than a biological ceiling.

Position paper v1.0 · gate cleared 2026-09-04
Paper 04 · Ultimate causes

The Evolved Substrate of Human Influenceability

The paper underneath the other three. Modern persuasion does not defeat reasoning, it goes around it, by showing evolved motivational systems stimuli those systems never met in the environment that shaped them.

The exploitable regularities are not bugs. They are adaptations running outside their design environment, which is why naming the system under a technique turns “this feels manipulative” into a question with an answer: which system is being triggered, and is the trigger honest?

Behavior & evidence

Claims about people, graded

Earlier work that audits widely taught claims about persuasion and group behavior against the published evidence, under the same grading rule.

Paper 05 · Evidence audit

The Closing Evidence Atlas

A pre-registered PRISMA 2020 systematic review and Bayesian meta-analysis of the 39 named techniques the sales-training industry teaches as settled craft. 11,785 records screened across seven public databases.

Fifteen of the thirty-nine have no peer-reviewed empirical study behind them at all. Exactly two clear the pre-registered threshold for a primary meta-analysis, both are message-framing effects near a third of a standard deviation, and neither is a closing technique in the sense the industry sells one.

Paper v1.0 · production run complete · zero divergent transitions
Paper 06 · Social dynamics

The Value Ledger

Why the same behaviours keep showing up in every human group. Twelve sections working from ultimate causes down to the interface most people actually operate on, which is impression management.

Value to the group is the master variable, and it cannot simply be claimed. That is why the machinery is built the way it is: honest signalling to make claims expensive, two separate routes to status, a distributed reputation ledger, and a defensive layer for catching people who fake all three.

Paper v1.0 · partial citation gate, stated on the page
Paper 07 · Companion brief

Conveying Value to the Tribe

The shorter companion to Paper 06. Opens with how an evolutionary explanation is supposed to work, including the adaptive-lag caveat and what separates a real one from a story, then walks seven mechanisms.

Cheap talk gets discounted, which is the whole reason value has to be shown rather than asserted. Warmth and competence are the two axes a person is read on before anything else is processed.
